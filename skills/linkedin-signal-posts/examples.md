# Voice examples

> **Replace these with your own accepted posts as you publish them.** The skill imitates what's in this file far more reliably than it obeys style rules. Two seed examples below, annotated.

## Example 1 — education impact angle

My TOK teacher had a complaint: students can define every Area of Knowledge and connect none of them to real life.

So I built a GPT that does one thing — it takes a knowledge question and forces you to argue it through real-world examples before it lets you near a conclusion. No essay writing. Just the thinking part.

Teachers at my school now use it in class. The surprising part: the students who benefit most aren't the strugglers, it's the strong writers who were coasting on structure instead of ideas.

Narrow tools that refuse to do the work for you beat general chatbots for learning. That's the whole finding.

**Why this works:** first line is the teacher's problem, not an announcement. Proof is "teachers use it in class" — real, specific, not a number that begs scrutiny. Ends on a transferable insight, not a CTA.

## Example 2 — builder story angle

Three weekends, one rule: the PDF never leaves your machine.

BytePDF compresses PDFs entirely in the browser. No upload, no server, no "we care about your privacy" page — the architecture is the privacy policy.

Building it taught me more about WebAssembly memory limits than any tutorial. The 100MB file that crashes the tab is a better teacher than the docs.

If you're a student or indie dev who moves PDFs around all day, it's free.

**Why this works:** the constraint is the hook. One concrete technical lesson gives non-users a reason to have read it. The CTA is one flat sentence, not a plea.
